Step 3: Creating the The Game Loop and Rendering the Player.
   - we will write commands to continuously clear old screen drawings and redraw our player character at their tracked coordinate positions
   - video games cannot just draw a picture once and sit still. Games work exactly like a traditional flip-book animation: they update positions
    and redraw everything on the screen roughly 60 times a second.

Step 4: Tracking Keyboard Inputs
- W (up), A (left), S (down), and D (right).
- we also have to keep track of whether each key is currently being held down
-  we can check if a key is held down during every single cycle of mainGameLoop

(In standard programming, when you hold down a keyboard key, there is a small built-in delay before your operating system starts 
repeating the key (like when you hold down a letter while typing a text message). If we used that default behavior,
 our player avatar would move a tiny bit, freeze for a split second, and then start stuttering across the screen!)

Step 5: Activating Real-Time Player Movement
- If the loop checks and finds that a keyPressed is currently true, it will add or subtract a tiny amount from the player's tracking position variables .
-mainGameLoop runs 60 times a second, checking our movement switches inside the loop means that the game instantly calculates new coordinates and re-renders the circle at its brand new location on every single frame.

Step 6: Enforcing Arena Boundaries
-We are going to add a safety check inside our mainGameLoop function.
 Before updating the player's position, we will check if the new position would push the circle past the edges of our canvas boundaries.
